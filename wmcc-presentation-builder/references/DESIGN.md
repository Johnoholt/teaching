## Template authority

Work on a copy of `../assets/presentation-template.pptx`. Reuse or duplicate its slides instead of rebuilding the design from memory.

- Slide 1 is the protected title-slide format. Replace its title and subtitle, but preserve the split panel, logo, colors, proportions, and corner geometry.
- Slide 2 is the protected outline or large-list format. Replace its title and list, but preserve the white canvas, green rail, vertical white logo, and established geometry.
- Slide 3 is the approved section-divider format. Replace its text while preserving the dark field, gray corner forms, white logo, divider rule, and green accent.
- Slides 4–10 are reusable layout examples. Duplicate the layout that fits the content, replace the demonstration material, and remove unused template slides from the finished deck.
- Do not include every sample layout merely because it exists. Let the source material determine the final slide sequence.

Preserve the template's master, layouts, theme, embedded logo artwork, and slide dimensions. Edit slide-level objects whenever possible. Do not modify a shared master unless the intended change belongs on every slide using it.

## Design character

The presentation should feel like a straightforward classroom PowerPoint with clear WMCC branding.

- Use the split gray-and-white title slide as the opening identity.
- Use white content slides with a narrow forest-green rail as the default.
- Use the dark section divider only for meaningful topic changes.
- Keep the composition flat, spacious, and editable.
- Use purple selectively for contrast, comparison, or alternating sequence steps.
- Favor readable teaching content over decorative styling.

## Slide canvas and alignment

### Canvas

- Use 16:9 widescreen at 13.333 × 7.5 inches.
- Preserve the template dimensions of 12,192,000 × 6,858,000 EMU.
- Do not resize or crop the slide canvas.

### Content-slide alignment

- Keep the green brand rail at the far left.
- Start ordinary slide titles approximately 1.1 inches from the left edge.
- Start body content approximately 1.5–1.7 inches from the left edge.
- Keep ordinary titles near the top and align repeated slide types consistently.
- Leave at least 0.45 inches of clear space at the right and bottom edges.
- Use no more than two main content columns on a standard slide.

### Object placement

- Align related text boxes, table edges, image frames, and chart bounds precisely.
- Keep visual evidence close to the text that explains it.
- Use a consistent top edge for columns and paired objects.
- Avoid casual offsets, uneven card grids, or objects floating without alignment.

## Color palette

| Role | Color | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Structural green | WMCC deep green | `#064834` | Content rail, native shapes, section accent |
| Logo green | WMCC logo green | `#004833` | Official logo artwork and compatible chart series |
| Secondary accent | WMCC purple | `#7B2B83` | Comparison headings, alternating process steps, restrained emphasis |
| Title gray | Medium gray | `#7F7F7F` | Title-slide field and geometric corner forms |
| Section background | Dark gray | `#3A3A3A` | Section-divider background |
| Light neutral | Light gray | `#E7E6E6` | Image placeholders, subtle bands, alternate table rows |
| Rule | Neutral gray | `#C8C8C8` | Dividers, process tracks, table borders, chart gridlines |
| Base | White | `#FFFFFF` | Standard content background |
| Primary text | Black | `#000000` | Body copy and ordinary titles |

### Color rules

- Preserve official logo colors. Do not recreate or recolor the logo.
- Use white as the normal content background.
- Use green as the main structural color.
- Use purple as a secondary accent, not the dominant slide color.
- Use dark gray only for section-divider slides.
- Do not introduce additional accent colors unless the subject requires them for meaning.
- Maintain strong text contrast on every background.

## Typography

### Typeface

- Use Calibri Light for major deck titles and section titles.
- Use Calibri for content titles, headings, body text, tables, and charts.
- Preserve the template's existing fonts when duplicating a source slide.
- Use one font family per role rather than adding decorative typefaces.

### Recommended sizes

| Element | Recommended size |
|---|---:|
| Deck title | 44–48 pt |
| Section title | 50–56 pt |
| Outline title | 44–48 pt |
| Standard content title | 34–38 pt |
| Column or subsection heading | 23–26 pt |
| Standard body text | 20–24 pt |
| Table and chart text | 15–18 pt |
| Caption or source cue | 12–15 pt |

### Typography rules

- Use black titles on white content slides and white titles on dark or gray fields.
- Keep titles direct and reasonably short.
- Do not place periods after short titles or headings.
- Avoid all-capital headings except established abbreviations.
- Use bold sparingly for headings or important terms.
- Do not shrink ordinary body text below 17 pt to force content onto a slide. Revise or divide the material instead.

## Branding and repeated elements

### Title slide

- Preserve the vertical split: gray field on the left and white field on the right.
- Preserve the green upper-left and lower-right corner forms.
- Keep the full-color WMCC logo on the white panel.
- Keep the title and subtitle centered within the gray panel.
- Replace only the title and subtitle unless the user explicitly requests another change.

### Content slides

- Preserve the narrow green rail along the far left edge.
- Preserve the vertical white WMCC logo near the bottom of the rail.
- Use a white background with no additional logo.
- Do not add a footer, slide number, or navigation label unless requested.

### Section-divider slide

- Preserve the dark gray field and the gray corner geometry.
- Preserve the white WMCC logo at the lower right.
- Use one large white title and an optional short white subtitle.
- Align the title and subtitle to the same left edge.
- Preserve the thin gray rule with the short green leading accent.
- Use section dividers only for real changes in topic.

### Logo restrictions

- Use the logo artwork embedded in the template or the bundled official files.
- Preserve its proportions and clear space.
- Do not rotate, stretch, crop, outline, recolor, or add effects to the logo.
- Do not place a second prominent logo on standard content slides.

## Content density

- Write for college students or a competent technical audience.
- Select and summarize the most important source material instead of transcribing it.
- Preserve definitions, procedures, distinctions, exceptions, examples, responsibilities, and important numbers.
- Prefer three or four meaningful bullets on an ordinary content slide.
- Allow bullets to wrap when explanation is necessary, but keep most to one or two lines.
- Use a short introductory sentence only when it helps frame the material.
- Avoid one-line slides except for a genuine transition.
- Divide crowded content before reducing readability or dropping important evidence.

## Layout catalog

### 1. Title slide

Duplicate template slide 1.

Include:

- Presentation title
- Brief subtitle, course, unit, or week label when useful

Do not add an agenda or several lines of administrative information to the title slide.

### 2. Outline or large list

Duplicate template slide 2.

- Use for an outline, learning objectives, a short agenda, or four compact topics.
- Retain the large, open layout.
- Use another content layout when the entries need explanation.

### 3. Section divider

Duplicate template slide 3.

- Use a short section title.
- Add no more than one short subtitle.
- Do not use the divider automatically after a fixed number of slides.

### 4. Standard content

Duplicate template slide 4 as the default teaching layout.

- Use a direct title.
- Add an optional framing sentence.
- Present three or four substantial bullets.
- Use for concepts, responsibilities, limitations, definitions, or explanatory summaries.

### 5. Two-column comparison

Duplicate template slide 5.

- Use for two concepts with a clear relationship or contrast.
- Give each column a short heading.
- Keep the thin center divider.
- Use green for the first heading and purple for the second when distinction helps.
- Do not surround each column with a card or panel.

### 6. Image and explanation

Duplicate template slide 6 and replace the gray image area with the actual image or screenshot.

- Use one primary image that adds instructional value.
- Keep the image at roughly 55–60 percent of the content width.
- Use up to three concise explanatory bullets beside it.
- Keep a short caption or source cue below the image only when useful.

### 7. Process or sequence

Duplicate template slide 7.

- Use native editable shapes and connectors.
- Four horizontal steps are a practical maximum for this format.
- Keep step headings short and explanations compact.
- Alternate green and purple only when it helps track the sequence.
- Use a different arrangement when the process has more than four substantial steps.

### 8. Comparison table

Duplicate template slide 8.

- Build a native PowerPoint table.
- Use a green header row with white text.
- Use thin neutral borders and restrained alternating gray rows.
- Adjust column widths to the actual text.
- Do not use a table when a short bullet list communicates the material more clearly.

### 9. Data or worked example

Duplicate template slide 9.

- Use a native editable chart, equation, table, or worked sequence.
- Keep the evidence on the left and interpretation on the right when that balance fits.
- Label units, axes, assumptions, and illustrative data clearly.
- Use verified or supplied values in real presentations.

### 10. Summary

Duplicate template slide 10 only when synthesis helps.

- Use three or four conclusions or next actions.
- Keep the standard green content rail.
- Use restrained numbered markers when sequence or priority matters.
- Do not add a generic “Thank You” slide unless requested.

## Images and visual evidence

- Use images that explain, document, or ground the subject.
- Prefer authentic source images, equipment photos, screenshots, diagrams, and relevant environments.
- Use only one primary image on most image-led slides.
- Crop intentionally without removing useful evidence.
- Preserve the proportions of photographs, screenshots, and logos.
- Do not reuse the same image on several slides unless the repetition is instructionally necessary.
- Keep labels and explanations editable rather than flattening them into the image.
- Put a compact image-source reference in the speaker notes.

## Tables, charts, and diagrams

### Tables

- Use native PowerPoint tables.
- Keep text large enough for projection.
- Use meaningful column widths and row heights.
- Preserve all required rows, columns, labels, and values.
- Avoid heavy borders or excessive color banding.

### Charts

- Use native editable PowerPoint charts.
- Use WMCC green for a single series and purple for a second series when needed.
- Use light neutral gridlines.
- Show data labels only when they improve comprehension.
- Avoid redundant chart titles when the slide title already names the subject.
- Check labels, scales, units, and signs against the source data.

### Diagrams

- Use simple native PowerPoint shapes for required editable diagrams.
- Keep connectors clear of text.
- Prefer horizontal or vertical connector routes.
- Keep diagrams subordinate to the teaching point.
- Do not build decorative illustrations from large collections of shapes.

## Speaker notes and references

Write notes as short prompts for live presentation.

### Notes style

- Use two to four brief cues on a typical slide.
- Use phrases or short sentences that can be scanned while speaking.
- Include reminders for examples, questions, cautions, emphasis, or transitions.
- Do not write a full narration script.
- Do not repeat the slide text unless the cue adds teaching value.

### References

- Add short source references in the speaker notes.
- Use a compact form such as organization or title, page or section, and URL when available.
- Do not require MLA, APA, or another formal style unless requested.
- Put citations on the visible slide only when the audience needs them or the source requires them.

## Editability requirements

- Keep titles, body text, labels, and captions as editable text.
- Keep bullet lists as native PowerPoint bullets.
- Keep tables as native PowerPoint tables.
- Keep charts as native PowerPoint charts with editable data.
- Keep required process diagrams and connectors editable.
- Keep formulas and worked-example steps editable when practical.
- Allow official logos, photographs, and source screenshots to remain image assets.
- Do not convert an entire slide into a screenshot.

## Patterns to avoid

- Restyling the protected title or outline formats
- Rebuilding the green rail or vertical logo when the template already provides them
- Floating card grids or dashboard-style panels
- Decorative text boxes around every point
- Adding large geometric corner forms to ordinary content slides
- Repeating the full-color logo on every slide
- One-line slides used for artificial drama
- Tiny body text used to preserve an overcrowded slide
- Images selected only to fill empty space
- Dense paragraphs that belong in notes or source material
- Generic slogans, vague claims, or inflated wording
- Flattened tables, charts, processes, or text

## Quality checks

Before delivering a presentation:

1. Confirm the slide size matches the template.
2. Confirm the title and outline formats retain their protected geometry and branding.
3. Confirm the current section-divider format is used without unapproved changes.
4. Confirm unused demonstration slides and placeholder text have been removed.
5. Confirm most slides use the simple white content format with the green rail.
6. Confirm every slide has a clear instructional purpose.
7. Confirm ordinary content slides provide reasonable coverage rather than one-line summaries.
8. Confirm body text is readable when projected.
9. Confirm bullets, tables, charts, diagrams, and labels remain editable.
10. Confirm chart labels, connectors, and table text do not overlap.
11. Confirm images retain correct proportions and useful content.
12. Confirm speaker notes are brief and useful while presenting.
13. Confirm external facts and images have short references in the notes.
14. Render and visually inspect every slide.
15. Correct crowding, clipping, awkward wrapping, inconsistent spacing, weak contrast, and distorted assets before delivery.
