# WMCC Presentation Builder

Create an instructional PowerPoint that preserves the source's important substance while remaining easy to scan, teach from, and edit.

Follow the built-in Presentations skill for authoring, rendering, validation, and delivery. Read `references/DESIGN.md` before planning layouts or styling a WMCC-branded deck. Work on a copy of `assets/presentation-template.pptx`; reuse its masters and duplicate the appropriate sample slides instead of rebuilding the design. Treat the template as a visual reference, not as a source of factual content.

## Choose the visual system

- Apply the bundled WMCC template by default.
- Preserve the title and outline slide formats. Replace their text without changing their panels, logos, proportions, colors, or geometric furniture.
- Use the approved dark section-divider format without additional restyling.
- Use slides 4–10 as a layout catalog. Duplicate only the needed layouts, replace their demonstration content, and remove unused template slides from the finished deck.
- If the user explicitly supplies another template or reference deck, follow that design instead while retaining this skill's content, notes, editability, and validation requirements.
- If a supplied deck contains source material but the user does not ask to copy its appearance, treat it as content only and keep the WMCC design.
- Use `assets/wmcc-logo.png` on light backgrounds and `assets/wmcc-logo-white.png` on dark backgrounds.

## Build the deck

### 1. Inspect the source material

- Identify the audience, purpose, desired length, and any required topics.
- Treat the supplied material as the factual authority unless the user requests external research.
- Record page, section, slide, sheet, or URL locations while reviewing the sources so references remain traceable.
- Ask a question only when missing information would materially change the deck. Otherwise, make a reasonable choice and proceed.

### 2. Select the instructional content

- Summarize and organize instead of transcribing.
- Preserve definitions, procedures, distinctions, exceptions, examples, safety points, responsibilities, and important numbers that the audience needs.
- Remove repetition, administrative filler, and detail that does not support the presentation's purpose.
- Do not invent claims, data, examples, or citations. Label illustrative material clearly.
- Use plain, direct language appropriate for college students or a competent technical audience.

### 3. Plan the slide sequence

- Give every slide one clear instructional purpose.
- Use a minimal title slide and meaningful section dividers only when the subject truly shifts.
- Make the standard content layout the default for most slides.
- Choose two-column, image-and-text, process, table, chart, worked-example, or summary layouts only when the material benefits from them.
- Prefer three or four substantive bullets on ordinary content slides. Allow explanation when needed, but split crowded slides before shrinking body text below 17 pt.
- Avoid one-line slides unless they mark a genuine transition.
- Avoid a generic agenda or closing slide unless the material or user requests one.

### 4. Author for editability

- Keep titles, body text, bullets, captions, tables, charts, equations, and required diagrams editable.
- Use native PowerPoint objects for tables, charts, processes, and connectors.
- Keep official logos, photographs, and source screenshots as image assets with their proportions preserved.
- Do not flatten whole slides or substitute decorative infographics for editable instructional content.
- Use one coherent composition per slide. Avoid card grids, dashboard styling, excessive icons, and decorative containers.

### 5. Write speaker notes

- Add two to four short cues to a typical slide.
- Write phrases or brief sentences that a presenter can scan while speaking.
- Use cues for examples, emphasis, cautions, questions, or transitions; do not write a narration script or merely repeat the slide.
- End with compact source references when applicable, using the organization or title plus page, section, slide, sheet, or URL. Formal citation styles are unnecessary unless requested.
- Associate each reference with the slide whose content it supports.

### 6. Check the result

- Confirm the deck covers the important source material without needless repetition.
- Confirm every factual claim is supported and every external fact or image has a short note reference.
- Confirm the slide size, colors, typography, logo treatment, and layouts follow `references/DESIGN.md` or the explicitly supplied template.
- Confirm required content remains editable.
- Render and visually inspect every slide. Correct clipping, overlap, awkward wrapping, tiny text, weak contrast, inconsistent spacing, distorted images, and unclear chart labels or connectors.
- Run the presentation validation workflow before delivery.
